Spiritual Integration

Where faith and technology become one path, not two

Introduction

Faith as a Professional Foundation

Elder Bednar's analogy of intertwined strands captures my experience perfectly: spiritual growth and professional development are not parallel lines that never meet — they are threads woven together, each making the other stronger. This page documents the spiritual materials that directly shape how I work, lead, and grow in IT.

"Gather together in one all things in Christ." — Elder David A. Bednar (2018) Bednar, D. A. (2018, November). Gather together in one all things in Christ. Ensign or Liahona.

Theme One

Faith & Guidance

Scripture

James 1:5

"If any of you lack wisdom, let him ask of God, that giveth to all men liberally…" This scripture is my first response to complex IT challenges. Before designing a cloud architecture or troubleshooting a critical production issue, I ask God for clarity of mind and direction.

General Conference Talk

Holland (2000) — "Cast Not Away Therefore Your Confidence"

Elder Holland's message strengthens my resolve to trust God's timing when pursuing career goals, even when progress feels slow. During setbacks in technical learning or job searching, this talk reminds me that God's silence is not His absence.

Professional Connection: When I face an unsolvable debugging session at 2 AM, I have learned to step away, pray, and return with renewed clarity. This is not superstition — it is a practiced discipline of trusting that divine wisdom supplements human effort.

Theme Two

Discipline & Consistency

Scripture

Alma 37:6

"By small and simple things are great things brought to pass." This is the principle behind both daily scripture study and CI/CD automation. Small, consistent commits to a pipeline — or to prayer — compound into extraordinary results over time.

Scripture

Doctrine & Covenants 64:33–34

"Be not weary in well-doing, for ye are laying the foundation of a great work." This verse speaks to the long slog of IT learning. Certification preparation, late study nights, and project iteration are the foundation of a great career — I must not quit.

Scripture

Doctrine & Covenants 58:27–28

"Men should be anxiously engaged in a good cause, and do many things of their own free will." This passage motivates me to take initiative in both spiritual and professional growth, rather than waiting to be assigned tasks.

Professional Connection: My CI/CD pipelines run on small, daily commits — just as my spiritual strength is built on small, daily practices. Spiritual discipline and DevOps discipline share the same underlying logic: consistency compounds.

Theme Three

Service & Leadership

Scripture

Mosiah 2:17

"When ye are in the service of your fellow beings ye are only in the service of your God." This is why I mentor two peers weekly. It is not a checkbox on a SMART goal — it is an act of worship. Every ticket I resolve for a frustrated user is a small act of divine service.

Scripture

Matthew 25:40

"Inasmuch as ye have done it unto one of the least of these my brethren, ye have done it unto me." This scripture transforms customer support from a burden into a calling. The most frustrated, least technical user deserves my full patience and skill.

Personal Insight

Debugging as a Spiritual Discipline

Debugging code has taught me patience, humility, and persistence. When a bug eludes me for hours, I am being trained in the same virtues required for spiritual growth: do not give up, ask for help, and examine your assumptions humbly.

Theme Four

Integration of Faith & Work

General Conference Talk

Bednar (2018) — "Gather Together in One All Things in Christ"

Elder Bednar's talk about integrating all aspects of life in Christ is the theological backbone of this portfolio. My GitHub commits, my AWS certifications, and my daily prayers are not separate efforts — they are one life consecrated to God.

Personal Reflection

The Portfolio as a Testimony

This portfolio is itself an act of integration. Each project description includes a spiritual reflection because I refuse to leave my faith at the door of the data center. My work testifies of my values, and my values are shaped by my faith.

Reflective Activity

Temple & Deep Reflection

For this reflective activity, I found a quiet and serene space to step away from screens, deadlines, and deployments — and simply think. I pondered the covenants I have made and the experiences that have brought me to this point in my IT journey. In that stillness, something became clear: the discipline I bring to my technical work and the discipline I bring to my spiritual life are not two separate efforts. They are one life, consecrated to a single purpose.

Elder Bednar's analogy of intertwined strands — drawn from his 2018 general conference address "Gather Together in One All Things in Christ" — speaks directly to what I experienced in that reflection. Just as individual strands are weak alone but form an unbreakable rope when woven together, my spiritual habits and my professional habits strengthen each other. My daily scripture study builds the same mental discipline that makes me a better debugger. My prayer life builds the same patience that makes me a better systems administrator when a production server goes down at midnight. They are not parallel tracks — they are the same track.

What brings me joy and peace, I discovered in that quiet space, is not the certifications or the Champion of the Week awards — though I am grateful for them. What brings me peace is knowing that every system I build, every student I support through the First Aid Hub, every device I maintain for church members, and every peer I mentor is an act of service to God. Mosiah 2:17 is not a scripture I recite — it is the architecture of my career. The insights from this reflective exercise have deepened my commitment to never separating who I am spiritually from what I do professionally. They are, as Elder Bednar taught, gathered together in one.

"Gather together in one all things in Christ." — Elder David A. Bednar (2018). The strands of spiritual growth and professional development are not separate — they are woven into one unbreakable purpose.
