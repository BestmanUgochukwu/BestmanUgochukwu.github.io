Spiritual Integration

Where faith and technology become one path, not two

Foundation

Faith as a Professional Foundation

Elder Bednar's analogy of intertwined strands captures my experience perfectly: spiritual growth and professional development are not parallel lines that never meet — they are threads woven together, each making the other stronger. This page documents the spiritual materials that directly shape how I work, lead, and grow in IT.

"Gather together in one all things in Christ." — Elder David A. Bednar (2018) Bednar, D. A. (2018, November). Gather together in one all things in Christ. Ensign or Liahona.

Theme One

Faith & Guidance

Scripture

James 1:5

"If any of you lack wisdom, let him ask of God, that giveth to all men liberally…" This scripture is my first response to complex IT challenges. Before designing a cloud architecture or troubleshooting a production issue, I ask God for clarity of mind and direction. Prayer is not my last resort — it is my first tool.

General Conference Talk

Holland (2000) — "Cast Not Away Therefore Your Confidence"

Elder Holland's message strengthens my resolve to trust God's timing when pursuing career goals, even when progress feels slow. During setbacks in technical learning — failed deployments, certification retakes, silent job applications — this talk reminds me that God's silence is not His absence. He is still working.

Professional Connection: When I face an unsolvable debugging session, I have learned to step away, pray, and return with renewed clarity. This is not superstition — it is a practiced discipline of trusting that divine wisdom supplements human effort.

Theme Two

Discipline & Consistency

Scripture

Alma 37:6

"By small and simple things are great things brought to pass." This is the principle behind both daily scripture study and CI/CD automation. Small, consistent commits to a pipeline — or to prayer — compound into extraordinary results over time. My five CompTIA certifications were not earned in a day; they were earned one chapter at a time.

Scripture

Doctrine & Covenants 64:33–34

"Be not weary in well-doing, for ye are laying the foundation of a great work." This verse speaks to the long slog of IT learning. Certification preparation, late study nights, and project iteration are the foundation of a great career. The Champion of the Week awards at CloudAdvisory were not luck — they were the visible result of invisible, daily consistency.

Scripture

Doctrine & Covenants 58:26–28 — Personal Agency

"Men should be anxiously engaged in a good cause, and do many things of their own free will, and bring to pass much righteousness." This passage is the theological foundation of my career philosophy. I did not wait to be assigned to build the First Aid Hub. I did not wait to be told to apply for the CloudAdvisory internship. Agency is not just a spiritual principle — it is a career strategy. Every meaningful achievement in my professional life came from choosing to act first.

Professional Connection — Agency: Reflecting on D&C 58:26–28 in a quiet moment of meditation, I realized that every role I have held — Technology Coordinator, DevOps intern, peer mentor, customer support specialist — was chosen. I was not drafted into any of them. And that choice is what makes the work meaningful. As a cloud engineer, I choose the architecture. As a leader, I choose to serve. As a professional, I choose to grow. Agency is the engine of both spiritual and professional development.

Theme Three

Service & Leadership

Scripture

Mosiah 2:17

"When ye are in the service of your fellow beings ye are only in the service of your God." This is why I mentor two peers weekly. It is not a checkbox — it is an act of worship. Every ticket I resolve for a frustrated user, every laptop I configure for a student, every question I answer for a junior IT learner, is a small act of divine service. Mosiah 2:17 is not a scripture I recite — it is the architecture of my career.

Scripture

Matthew 25:40

"Inasmuch as ye have done it unto one of the least of these my brethren, ye have done it unto me." This scripture transforms customer support from a burden into a calling. At Springboard, the most frustrated, least technical student deserves my full patience and skill. Building the First Aid Hub for BYU Pathway students was Matthew 25:40 in code — every feature was designed for the person who was struggling, not the person who had it all figured out.

Personal Insight

Debugging as a Spiritual Discipline

Debugging code has taught me patience, humility, and persistence. When a bug eludes me for hours, I am being trained in the same virtues required for spiritual growth: do not give up, ask for help, and examine your assumptions humbly. The moment you assume you know where the bug is, you stop looking — the same pride that ends debugging sessions ends spiritual growth.

Theme Four

Integration of Faith & Work

General Conference Talk

Bednar (2018) — "Gather Together in One All Things in Christ"

Elder Bednar's talk about integrating all aspects of life in Christ is the theological backbone of this portfolio. My GitHub commits, my AWS certifications, my daily prayers, and my peer mentoring sessions are not separate efforts — they are one life consecrated to God. The strands of spiritual and professional growth, woven together, form something stronger than either could be alone.

Professional–Spiritual Alignment

How My Competencies Align with My Beliefs

Cloud security mirrors my spiritual commitment to protecting what is entrusted to me. DevOps automation mirrors my belief in consistent, disciplined daily practice. Systems administration mirrors stewardship — caring for infrastructure that others depend on. Customer support mirrors service as worship. Every professional competency I have developed is a technical expression of a spiritual value I already held. They were never separate.

Reflective Activity — Personal Agency

Temple & Deep Reflection

For this reflective activity, I found a quiet and serene space — away from screens, deployment pipelines, and deadlines — to meditate on Doctrine and Covenants 58:26–28 and its teaching on personal agency. I pondered the covenants I have made and the choices that have shaped my path to this point. In that stillness, one truth surfaced clearly: every significant thing I have accomplished came from a decision to act, not a response to being told what to do.

The Lord's counsel to be "anxiously engaged in a good cause" and to "do many things of their own free will" is not just spiritual instruction — it is a blueprint for professional excellence. I chose to apply for the CloudAdvisory internship without being pushed. I chose to build the First Aid Hub for BYU Pathway students without being assigned. I chose to earn five CompTIA certifications through personal discipline, not because a professor required them. Agency is the reason those achievements are meaningful. They were mine to make.

Elder Bednar's analogy of intertwined strands deepened this reflection significantly. As I sat quietly, I realized that my spiritual discipline and my professional discipline are not two separate ropes running alongside each other — they are two strands of a single rope. My daily scripture study builds the same mental focus and patience that makes me a better cloud engineer. My prayer life builds the same calm under pressure that makes me a better systems administrator when a production server fails at midnight. The strand of faith and the strand of technical skill reinforce each other. Neither is as strong without the other.

What brings me deepest joy and peace, I discovered in that quiet space, is not the Champion of the Week certificates or the certifications hanging on the wall — though I am genuinely grateful for them. What brings me peace is the knowledge that every system I build, every student I serve through the First Aid Hub, every device I maintain for church members, and every peer I mentor in IT is an act of consecration. I am not building a career and a testimony in parallel. I am building one life, offered to God through the work He has prepared me to do.

"Men should be anxiously engaged in a good cause, and do many things of their own free will, and bring to pass much righteousness." — Doctrine and Covenants 58:27. Agency is not just a spiritual gift. It is the engine of every meaningful professional achievement.
